Calf EMG Front End

A surface EMG amplifier on a single 3.3 V supply, made to feed the ESP32-S3 ADC. Electrodes on the calf go through an instrumentation amp, a 20 Hz high-pass, a 500 Hz anti-alias low-pass and an AC-coupled final gain stage. The output sits at 1.65 V, the middle of the ADC range.

Total gain ≈ 1,110 Passband 20 – 500 Hz Output bias 1.65 V ≈ 2,180 counts Sample at 2 kHz

Design math

High-pass corner (equal C)f = 1 / (2π · 100n · √(56k · 110k)) = 20.3 Hz, Q = 0.70
Low-pass corner (equal R)f = 1 / (2π · 10k · √(47n · 22n)) = 495 Hz, Q = 0.73
Total gain (in the passband)(1 + 100k/10k) · (1 + 100k/1k) = 11 · 101 = 1,111
Stage 4 DC block (Rg + Cg)f = 1 / (2π · 1k · 47µ) = 3.4 Hz, well below 20 Hz; DC gain = 1
Why Cg is needed: worst-case MCP6004 offset, 3 sections × ±4.5 mVwithout Cg: ±13.5 mV · 101 ≈ ±1.36 V shift · with Cg: ±13.5 mV
ADC scale (ESP32-S3, 12 dB attenuation ≈ 0–3.1 V)3.1 V / 4096 ≈ 0.76 mV per count · 1.65 V ≈ 2,180 counts
Standing calf EMG, about 50 µV RMS at the skin50 µV · 1,111 ≈ 56 mV → ≈ 73 ADC counts RMS
Headroom before clipping±1.6 V / 1,111 ≈ ±1.4 mV at the electrodes

Build and check

  • With the electrodes unplugged, GPIO1 should sit at about 1.65 V (U1D pin 14 within a few mV of VREF). The raw reading is roughly 2,100–2,200; the exact number varies by chip, and the web app removes this DC level, so it doesn't need to be 2048.
  • If pin 14 sits far from 1.65 V, check that Cg is in place between Rg and VREF.
  • If the INA333 output (pin 6) is stuck near 0 V or 3.3 V, the electrode DC offset is saturating it. Check the electrode contact first, then raise RG to 22k (gain 5.5).
  • If the standing signal looks small in the app, raise Rf to 220k (gain about 2,400). If strong contractions clip, lower it.
  • Run on battery only. Don't charge the laptop or plug it into mains power while the electrodes are on your skin.
  • Keep the electrode leads short and twisted together. Long loose leads pick up 60 Hz hum.
